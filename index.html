Left to right is frequency (log scale, 20 Hz to 20 kHz). Up is how loud each frequency is right now, in decibels. Purple marks frequencies both sounds contain at the same moment; "Shared now" lists where both are louder than −60 dB. Two pure tones barely share any frequencies, while recordings, voices and noise share a lot. Nearby tones can still mask each other because the ear blurs neighbouring frequencies, shown in the next plot.

Overlap of the smaller pattern Shared

Horizontal axis is frequency on a log scale (20 Hz to 20 kHz, roughly the human range). Each tone is a bump about one critical band wide (Glasberg & Moore ERB). An uploaded recording is analysed from its middle section and smoothed to critical-band resolution; while it plays, the plot follows the sound live. Bands widen at high frequencies, so sounds there need to be farther apart to escape masking.
